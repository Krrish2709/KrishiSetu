The crisis

Why bank credit
keeps missing
India's farmers.

01 · Collateral

Land titles decide who borrows.

Seven in ten agricultural households work less than a hectare, and tenant and oral-lease farmers have no title to pledge. Underwriting built on collateral leaves them to moneylenders charging 24–60%.

02 · Calendar

EMIs ignore the crop cycle.

Cash arrives once, 14–21 days after the harvest is sold. Monthly instalments fall due when the field has nothing to sell, so a sound farmer looks like a bad borrower.

03 · Climate

One weak monsoon becomes a default.

The 2026 southwest monsoon closed at 87% of its long-period average, the fourth-lowest since 2001. In 316 of 741 districts rainfall was deficient or worse. Without parametric cover, weather risk lands on the lender and the farmer.

Our approach

An intelligent credit layer
between lenders and farmers.

KrishiSetu is a B2B2C Lending Service Provider under RBI's Digital Lending Guidelines. Banks and NBFCs lend. We originate, underwrite and monitor the loan, and the farmer pays no platform fee.

01

Agri-Credit Score

An eight-factor scorecard, normalised against each crop's state benchmark. It is trained on a portfolio calibrated to official statistics and re-estimated on real repayment outcomes.

02

Crop-cycle credit

Funds go out at sowing and fall due 14 days after the harvest sale. A verified drought or flood triggers a 90-day moratorium automatically.

03

Parametric insurance

Triggers on IMD rainfall and satellite NDVI, layered on PMFBY. Claims pay off the loan before it can default.

04

No-tech access

IVR in 12 languages, KrishiMitra village agents and Common Service Centres. Aadhaar eKYC and SMS disbursement mean no smartphone is needed.

05

Scheme stack

Enrolment in KCC, PMFBY, PM-KISAN and e-NAM at origination. Each scheme unlocks benefits and improves the score.

06

Blended risk

Risk is shared across PMFBY, the parametric insurer, NABSanrakshan guarantees and lenders' priority-sector books. MISS brings the farmer's effective rate to 4%.

    The score · 1

    Eight factors, judged against the farmer's own agronomy.

    A Punjab wheat grower and a Vidarbha cotton grower are each compared with their own state benchmark, never with each other. The score uses no caste, religion or gender inputs.

    The score · 2

    Weights are learned, not asserted.

    A logistic scorecard is fitted on 150,000 simulated borrowers whose income, debt, irrigation and landholding match NSS, Agriculture Census and Land Use statistics state by state. The original expert weights act as a Bayesian prior.

    The score · 3

    Repayment behaviour and cash-flow capacity lead.

    Yield, weather and price matter mostly through the cash they generate. The model therefore weights the debt-service coverage of this crop cycle heavily, alongside the farmer's repayment record.

    The score · 4

    Every point maps to a probability of default.

    The score is scaled so that 650 means 19:1 good-to-bad odds (a 5% probability of default) and every 50 points doubles the odds. Lenders read one number on a scale they already know.

    How to read this dashboard

    Regional Risk Index (0–100)

    Built only from official data: debt load, how widespread indebtedness is, rain-fed exposure, fragmentation, the monsoon-2026 deficit, insurance gap and income. Weights come from principal-component analysis, and 100 is the riskiest major state.

    Avg KrishiSetu score (300–850)

    The trained scorecard applied to a simulated portfolio of farmers whose income, debt, landholding and irrigation match that state's statistics, averaged over holdings.

    Default rate · baseline vs monsoon 2026

    Expected one-cycle default under typical weather, and again conditioned on this season's observed district rainfall deficits. The gap is the stress the season adds.

    Credit gap

    Share of operational holdings without an operative Kisan Credit Card (KCC accounts as of March 2025 ÷ Agriculture Census holdings). It is the size of the opportunity, not a risk measure.
